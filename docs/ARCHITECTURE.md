# Architecture in detail

The long description of each module, moved out of CLAUDE.md so it isn't read in every session. Read the part you need before working on a module. The code and its doc comments are the source of truth: if this file disagrees with them, trust the code (and fix this file).

## Asset scripts

- `npm run card-art -- images/<name>.png [maxWidth]`: make a card painting, `src/assets/cards/<name>.webp` (480px wide unless `maxWidth` says otherwise), from a large original PNG or JPEG; name the original after the card's template id (`images/medic.png` for Médico) and the card shows it with no code change (`CardArt`'s `PAINTINGS`, checked by `CardArt.test.tsx`); an image without transparency (any JPEG) has its drawn-in checkerboard background removed, or its white background faded out when its border is all white, or a flat darker grey background (its border all one colour) taken out with a thin soft edge. The originals live in `images/` at the root, which git ignores
- `npm run mix-paintings -- <left> <right> <out>`: a painting for an action with none of its own yet, from two in `src/assets/cards`: each trimmed to its drawing and set side by side, standing on the bottom edge (`explosives-mix` for the Explosives fire option)
- `npm run icons`: regenerate the app icon PNGs from `public/icons/icon.svg`
- `npm run add-scenario -- [--check] [--replace] <file>`: add a map exported from Mis mapas ("Exportar", `<id>.m44map.json`) to the catalog, so every device has it once a push to `main` deploys it. It loads the app's code through Vite (`runnerImport`), checks the file (`catalogFile` in `data/catalog.ts`: `parseScenario`, not a built-in's id; `--check` stops there), writes `src/data/catalog/<id>.json` as exported (id, terrain and units unchanged, so a game in progress on the device's copy goes on with the catalog's; `formatJson`; `--replace` updates one already there, keeping its `notes` if the file has none), adds it to `scripts/boards.json` and paints its art with `npm run board` (with `"drawn": ["river"]` when no river tile fits a bend). A map bigger than 13×9, or with a river fork no tile fits, isn't painted: its entry and image are removed and the app draws its art, as for Mis mapas. The description, `notes` and tests for special rules are added by hand
- `npm run board [-- <part of a name>]`: build the scenarios' board art from their terrain, every board listed in `scripts/boards.json` (its terrain file in `src/data/boards/`, which `scenarios.ts` imports too, or a catalog scenario's file, whose `tiles` and `pickedTiles` it reads, standard boards only; the image to write, `drawn`: terrain to draw in code instead of painting it, `river` (with its bridges; hedgerows and lakes are always drawn), and `separateHills`: each hill on its own tile instead of joined into ridges). The base is the empty board (`scripts/board-art/empty-board.webp`, its grid measured at the top of the script) cropped to the edges of its hex grid, the box the app lays the image in, built at its full size (2435×1514) and saved at the app's (1621×1008, `APP_ART_HEIGHT`). Painted terrain comes from the tile library, `scripts/board-art/tiles/<terrain>/`: one hex per image, filling its width, at any resolution; each hex gets one of its terrain's tiles, turned 60° a random number of times (seeded by the hex). A river tile's name starts with the edges its river joins (0 = east, clockwise: `3-0.webp` runs west to east, `0-2-4.webp` forks, `3.webp` ends in a pond): a river joining three river hexes forks, one joining one runs into a lake beside it, else ends in a pond if it stops on the board, else carries straight on, the first a tile fits (`riverPlanner`). A lake is painted from `tiles/lake/` (named by the edges its water carries on through, `-r<edges>` for a river flowing in: `0-1-5-r3.webp`) when every hex of it has a piece that fits, and drawn otherwise. Water tiles have no grass or bevel of their own, only the water and a narrow bank (`npm run water-tile -- <tile> <library tile>` makes one from a whole hex). A beach (`tiles/beach/`) and a swamp fade out along a ragged line into the land beside them (`JoinedHex.fade`), a beach tile whose name starts with edges (its grass tufts there) turned to face it, or one named `surf-<edges>` turned to face the ocean beside it. An ocean hex (`tiles/ocean/`) next to land takes the tile whose shore best faces it (`4-5.webp`: the land across edges 4 and 5, `tilesFacing`), else open sea (`sea.webp`), only turned 180° so its waves stay level. By about one in three of a forest's edges onto the countryside, a lone tree or two stand out on it (`SPILLS`, a `spill` hex laid over everything else, `spillMaker`) and it's turned to join the river's edges; a painted bridge is a river tile with `board-art/pieces/bridge.webp` laid across it. A hill is made into a ridge (`scripts/joined-hills.mjs`): the plateau runs out through each edge it shares with another hill or a river (the lakes' outline, `shoreCurves` in `src/boardArt/terrainTiles.mjs`, with a wider neck, bulging only a little way into a river), the hill tile's cliff ring is unrolled along it, the plateau is laid across the whole board so it carries over the hex lines, and a little past the cliffs' foot the hill is clear, so the board's countryside shows round it; a lone hill is made the same way. Forests next to their own kind blend into it (`src/boardArt/joinedTiles.mjs`): on each shared edge the tile's bevel is replaced by the tile mirrored in from just inside it, with a faint hex line, and a forest grows tree crowns (cut from the tile, found by their lit middles) across it, the same crowns drawn by both hexes. A town keeps its painted tile as it is, turned by the hex, its outside fading into the countryside. A town's tile is picked by the hex, or in the map editor (`Scenario.pickedTiles`, planBoard's `picked`). Out over open countryside (`bare` edges, a town's, a river's or a hedgerow's countryside included), a forest grows more crowns, and patches of its ground spill over some edges in lumpy yards: an overflow image half as large again as the hex, laid on last. Towns and rivers (painted ones through the same maker, drawn ones after they're drawn) end in the board's countryside: their tile's grass is kept only near the cobbles, houses or water (a town's through its own maker, `toCountryside` too), out to a wandering edge, and is clear beyond (`toCountryside`); each neighbour reaches into a river hex only by the edge it's next to: a forest (`woods`) grows in up to the water in the part of the hex facing it (`growWoods`), each tree whole (kept clear of the water and the hex's edge); a hill (`hillShapes`) runs on in as its own outline drawn from where it is (`hillOver`, `growHill`), its cliffs closing short of the water. Hedgerows are always painted from one approved field image (`src/assets/board-art/hedgerow-field.webp`, turned 180° for every other seed), each kept inside its own hex. The bridge piece is laid at `BRIDGE_WIDTH` of its width. Lakes, and drawn rivers and bridges, come from `src/boardArt/terrainTiles.mjs` (a river flowing through the edges it shares with the next river or bridge hexes, a lake joining its lake neighbours); the board's edges are made soft (`src/boardArt/softLines.mjs`; it also grades the empty board's pale print into a darker, fuller green first, `gradeCountryside`, so the app's `empty-board.webp` is graded too): the empty board's dark frame round the grid is filled with the countryside beside it (mirrored out across the edge hexes, so a map ends in countryside; the app's `empty-board.webp` has it too), and the hex lines (printed, and the tiles' rims) are blended with the board mirrored in from just inside each hex so 30% of the line is left, and the frame round the grid is put back over the new hexes. The barbed wire and sandbags pieces aren't part of the board art (they change in the game): they're `src/assets/pieces/*.webp`, drawn on the hex by `BarbedWireArt` and `SandbagsArt`, each image the hex's box with the piece where it sits. The terrain carries on into the half hexes just outside the board that the art's box shows (the zigzags above and below, the ends of the short rows: `outskirtKeys`, keys like `-1-4`), so a forest or a river doesn't stop dead at the edge: planBoard's `outskirts` gives each the terrain most of its board neighbours have and runs a river on through the edge it flows out of; nothing of them comes back onto the board (no lone trees), the frame is kept off them, and they're only art (nothing can stand there). Which art each hex gets (tile, turn, river edges, lake edges, ridges) is planned in `src/boardArt/boardPlan.mjs`, shared with the app; the script also writes the app's smaller empty board, `src/assets/board-art/empty-board.webp`

## Modules

- `src/game-core/`: plain TypeScript game logic with **no React imports**:
  - `BoardManager`: hex grid (with `bothSides`, both sides' units and every sandbag, for the map editor), Axis flip, pathfinding (`calculatePossibleMovesWithPaths`; a hedgerow is only entered from the hex the move starts on, and a unit leaving one moves 1 hex)
  - `Hex`: its terrain (`getType`, and `getTerrain`, the terrain's rules from `data/terrain.ts`; on a map with `Scenario.fordableRivers` a river's rules are `FORDABLE_RIVER`: any unit wades in and stops, can fire with `FORD_FIRE_DICE`, `FireContext.fromFord`, and is fired at as in the open; the editor's "Ríos vadeables" switch, `setFordableRivers`), barbed wire (`wire`, from `Scenario.wire`: removable, so not a terrain type; it stops any unit that enters, and a tank that enters removes it, `WIRE_CLEARING_UNIT_TYPES`, kept as `Order.clearedWire` or on the take-ground move for undo), sandbags (`sandbags`: this side's units' only, from `Scenario.sandbags` and Fortify; the enemy's can't be known, so firing asks. `GameSession` clears them when the unit moves off, keeping them on the order or battle edit for undo), board section (`_setSide`), neighbors (offset↔axial coordinates)
  - `GameSession`: owns one player's game (board, the side's command cards and their cooldowns, coins (supplies), each unit's supplies and luck, turn flow) and is the only place game rules run. Actions (`dropUnit`, `undoDrop` and `finishParadrop` for a scenario's paradrop (PARADROP, before the first turn: the player taps where each paratrooper landed on the table), `pickCard` (one command card or none, `null`) and `unpickCard` (back to the cards before any order or mark), `getActions` (a unit's actions and why it can't be given each one), `getMoveOptions`, `giveAction` (an action and the hex it moves to; a unit whose order holds gets the new one in its place), `issueOrder` (a plain move turned into its plain action, for the computer), `undoLastOrder`, `markHex`/`undoMarker` (the card's hexes, `markerRules.ts`), `giveRegroup`/`regroupPartners` (Regroup: the order's `partner`, the unit next to it whose figures join it, which gets no order of its own), `commitOrders` (pays the actions, carries out what they do and locks the orders, straight to MOVEMENT), `startBattle`, `endBattle`, `endTurn` (energy back, the card's cooldown starts), plus the map sync `removeUnit` (with the experimental `artilleryCrew` rule, `GameSetup.artilleryCrew`, a menu "Ajustes" switch, saved: artillery removed in the final phase leaves an infantry unit on its hex, `leavesCrew`, the edit's `replacement`), `relocateUnit` and `undoBattleEdit` (`canEditMap`: in the final phase, and in Órdenes before any order or mark, to fix a map that doesn't match the table; those edits are `beforeOrders` and can't be undone after the orders), `placeReinforcement` for the Reinforcements card (its unit arrives on the cross as the final phase starts; `placeReinforcement` when the cross was taken; an `add` battle edit), `makeFinalMove` (Behind Enemy Lines' move after the battle in pass and play, a `move` edit with `reposition`), and firing: `fireOptions` (the unit's fire options and what it lacks for each), `fireTargetsFor` (the hexes a unit can fire at, with an option), `fireAt` (at a hex picked on the map; `fire` takes the questionnaire's answers directly), `fireCollision`, `undoShot`, `canTakeGround`/`takeGround`/`undoTakeGround` (after a close assault with a hit or a flag, armour (never infantry) takes ground: it moves to the target's hex as a battle edit and gets one more shot, adjacent only; `Shot.tookGround`, derived in the summary), `keepResults` (apply fewer results than were rolled: `Shot.kept`, read with `appliedFaces`), `skipUnmovedFire`, `removeWire` (infantry on barbed wire removes it instead of firing: a 0-dice `Shot` with `removedWire`; firing from wire costs it a die, `WIRE_FIRE_DICE`; pass and play's `PassAndPlayMatch.removeWire`, and `settleWire`, which takes wire one side removed off the other's board (`wireRemovedByEnemy`) once the orders are carried out) and `removeWireAt`, `removeSandbagsAt` (map edits to match the table), `attackHex`/`undoCardAttack` (an attack card's roll per marked hex, before any unit fires), and in pass and play `sufferHits` (hits, flags (a point of infantry's morale each) and Take Cover's ignored hits), the camouflage badge (`camouflaged`, kept from turn to turn until the unit moves or battles; the enemy's: `EnemyUnit.camouflaged` and the fire question `camouflage`)) return `false` and change nothing when they aren't allowed. After each change it publishes an immutable `GameSnapshot` (with `cards`, `supplies`, `changes`: what this turn changed on each unit, and `turnEnd`: the energy back and the cards off cooldown). **Test mode** (`GameSetup.testMode`, the menu's "Modo prueba" switch, saved): plenty of supplies each turn and no cooldown
  - `actionCard.ts`: the actions game's command cards (`ActionCard`: cost, painting, an optional `marker` rule for hexes to mark, `needs` air or big guns, and its `effect`: Finest Hour's `extraDice`, `resupply`, `refuel`, an `attack` rolled per marked hex, `reinforcements`); the cards are in `data/actionCards.ts`. A side holds every card the scenario gives it from the start (`cardsOf`: `sideHasCard` by the scenario's air sorties and big guns, `cardCost` with `Scenario.cardCosts`, set in the map editor), plays one a turn or none, and waits `CARD_COOLDOWN_TURNS` before playing it again (`turnsLeft`, `isReady`; `GameSession.cardsPlayedOn`, saved). `unitAction.ts`: the `UnitAction` and `FireOption` types; the actions and fire options are data in `data/unitActions.ts` (cost, move, whether it ignores terrain, how it fires, an effect, whether own tablets offer it), the supplies' numbers in `data/unitSupplies.ts` and luck's in `data/luck.ts`. Docs: [ACTIONS_PLAN.md](ACTIONS_PLAN.md)
  - `orderRules.ts`: the orderable units (no order yet, and an action they can be given) and `MoveLimits`. `actionRules.ts` (the actions mode): `actionRefusal` (why a unit can't be given an action: another type's, not on own tablets, morale 0 (only Morale Up), a supply or equipment it lacks, no ammo or shells for one that fires), `moveLimitsOf`/`moveRulesOf`/`shotsOf` from the action's data (`data/unitActions.ts`), `plainActionForMove` (`PLAIN_ACTIONS`: the action a plain move turns into, for `GameSession.issueOrder` and the computer), and the fire options (`fireOptionRefusal`, `plainFireOption`: a shot with nothing picked and what a collision spends, `canFireAShot`). `unitSupplies.ts`: a unit's supplies as values (`fullSupplies`, `pay`, `refund`, `reload`, `refill`, `endOfTurn`); `GameSession` keeps each unit's, pays the orders' costs in `commitOrders` and carries out what the actions and cards do there (`carryOut`: Reload, Morale Up, Fortify's sandbags, Medic, Mechanic and Regroup in pass and play (`regroup`: `data/figures.ts` `regroupFigures` and `unitSupplies.ts` `betterOf`; a giver left with no figures leaves with a `remove` edit marked `regrouped`, which `unitsLost` doesn't count, and `PassAndPlayMatch.opponentOf` still shows it until the reveal, `leftByRegroup`); `refillByCard`: Resupply, Refuel; Take Cover in `sufferHits`, Camouflage in `endTurn`, Behind Enemy Lines' move in `finalMove`; on own tablets the table does Medic, Mechanic, Take Cover and that move, `tableReminders`), takes a point of infantry's morale per flag in `sufferHits`, rolls every shot and collision with the unit's luck (`luck.ts`: `rollWithLuck`, one draw per die; card attacks roll without it), pays each shot's fire option as it's rolled (a `Shot` keeps its `option` and what it `spent`, given back by `undoShot`) and gives energy back in `endTurn` (more to a unit that rested, none to one that moved: `turnSpent`). A unit with nothing left to fire has no shots left (`summarizeOrders`' `outOfAmmo`); the action's `fire.round` puts it in the firing order (Behind Enemy Lines `firesFirst`, No Respite with the units that didn't move).
  - `ai/`: the computer player for a pass and play match (the menu's "Contra el ordenador", `MatchSetup.computer`; `useComputerPlayer` in `MatchView` plays its side at once outside the battle, and `SharedBattleView` drives it in the battle, one thing at a time after `COMPUTER_PAUSE_MS`, its shot up until the person taps "Seguir") ([docs/PLAY_THE_COMPUTER.md](docs/PLAY_THE_COMPUTER.md)): `aiStep(match, side, brain)` does the side's next action through the same session and match actions a person's taps call (`false` when it has nothing to do, or the step is one both players share: the reveal, the movement and collisions, ending the battle), and a `Brain` picks among the options, seeing the enemy only as a player on that side would (`seenEnemies`, from `opponentOf`). A unit with nothing left to fire reloads, whatever the brain; a card whose hexes can't all be marked is given up for the turn. `basicBrain` is the very basic one (no card, plain actions), `standardBrain` the one the app plays (`makeStandardBrain(STANDARD_WEIGHTS)`: hexes scored by the shots they give against the fire they draw, terrain cover counted, the nearness of the enemy and of the objectives; shots by the share of the target they'd destroy; and from `actionChoice.ts` the card worth the most if it's worth playing (`chooseCard`, `chooseMark`), each unit's action for its move (`chooseAction`: Morale Up, Medic, Mechanic, Fortify, Bombardment, rest when tired, keeping the last fuel; else the plain action) and each shot's fire option (`chooseFireOption`: Explosives in close assault, Suppressive at a unit on an objective)). `selfPlay.ts` plays a match brain against brain for tests: `aiStep.test.ts` plays every built-in scenario without getting stuck, and `duel.test.ts` (only with `AI_DUEL=1`) counts the games one brain wins against another (`AI_TUNE=1` searches the weights)
  - `Unit` (`elite`, from `Scenario.elite`: a scenario's badge, drawn as a star; it moves and fires as its type does and carries more supplies), `Order` (its action's id, its shots; `canFire` is `shots > 0`; an order's index identifies it for the turn)
  - `position.ts`: `samePosition`, `positionKey` ("row-col") and `includesPosition`; use them rather than comparing rows and columns by hand
  - `fogOfWar.ts`: fog of war in pass and play and against the computer (`MatchSetup.fogOfWar`, the menu's "Niebla de guerra" switch in Ajustes, on by default; `PassAndPlayMatch.fog`, saved with `sighted`): `inSight`, the enemy units a side's units see (`VISION` hexes, `VISION_FROM_HILL` from a hill, in line of sight, enemy units blocking it), and `seenHexes`, the hexes they see. The match works out each side's sight (`sightOf`): against the computer live from the move to the end of the battle (and the units that fired at it this turn, and in the battle the artillery ordered to fire), and frozen at the end of the battle (`sighted`) until the next move; none before the first battle, when both armies are known; between two people only in the battle. `fogOn(side)` is the fog on a map drawn from that side's board, `Board`'s `fog` (`OpponentContext`'s on the game screen's maps): the dark hexes (the person's unseen ones against the computer; between two people those either side doesn't see) and that side's units left off (those its enemy doesn't see, when the enemy is a person); the battle draws the computer's board with all the person's units (`opponentOf(…, { fogged: false })`). `opponentOf` keeps only the seen units (and their orders, sandbags and camouflage), so the maps and the computer (`seenEnemies`) know only those; `enemiesOf` marks the rest `EnemyUnit.hidden`, so they aren't targets (`GameSession.seenEnemies`, `enemyAt`) but still block retreats and moves, and an attack card hits them (`enemyAt(…, { hidden: true })`)
  - `boardSize.ts`: a board's size (`Scenario.board`, omitted for the standard 13×9; up to `BOARD_LIMITS`, rows always odd so the board turned round for the Axis keeps its long rows), `isOnBoard` and `boardPositions`. `turnedRound` takes the size. Bigger maps are played in pass and play (and against the computer) only for now: the menu offers no own-tablet game for one. Their art is drawn by `drawBoard.ts`: each hex one of the empty board's, picked away from its printed section lines, its frame stretched round them and the section lines drawn where the thirds meet
  - `scenarioDraft.ts`: the map editor's edits as pure functions on a `Scenario` (`paintTerrain`, `placeUnit`, `removeUnit`, `toggleElite`, `toggleSandbags`, `setWire`, `setObjective`/`toggleObjective` (a side's objective, one hex each, named "Objetivo N" until renamed), `clearHex`, `resizeBoard` (an edge grown or shrunk by a column or two rows, new hexes painted with a terrain, everything moved along; `BoardSizeDialog`): a new map, the same one when nothing changes, `false` when not allowed), `strokeOf` (what a tool does over one stroke of the finger, decided on its first hex), the scenario's settings (`setText`, `setAttacker`, `setHandSize`, `setAirSorties`, `setBigGuns`, `setExtraDraws`, `setParadrop`, `setVictory`, `setVictoryMedals`, `setObjectiveName`, `removeObjective`, within `SETTING_LIMITS`; air sorties and big guns are left out while both sides have the usual ones), `newDraft`/`copyDraft`/`newMapId`, ids (a map's id is its name's slug and a random part, `bocage-3f9k`, kept once the map has left the editor named: `slugOf`, `mapIdFor`; a new map is `map-` and the random part until then, `isProvisionalId`), and undo (`recordStep`, with `amend` for a stroke or one setting changed again, `undoStep`, `redoStep`)
  - `scenarioFile.ts`: a scenario as a plain JSON file (`ScenarioFile`, `toScenarioFile`; the format is described in `scenarioFile.schema.json`, kept in step by its test), what the map editor will save and the catalog keeps. `validateScenario` lists what keeps a scenario from being played (`ScenarioProblem`: a `Localized` message and its hexes); `parseScenario` reads unknown JSON or throws a `ScenarioFileError` listing everything wrong. Every scenario, built-in or not, passes `validateScenario` (`scenarios.test.ts`)
  - `dice.ts` (the dice, `DieKind` and `SIDES_OF`, with Fuego de supresión's two (`suppressed`, `isEightSided`): the Memoir '44 battle die: 2 infantry, tank, grenade, supply (the star of the official die), flag; the house 8-sided long-range die (2 tanks, 3 infantry, flag, 2 supplies), a per-game setting (`GameSetup.longRangeDie`, `GameSession.longRangeDie`) rolled at targets that aren't adjacent; and the attack cards' die, with a second grenade for the supply. `ShotTarget.die` says which was rolled) and `turnSummary.ts` (what each order means for the battle, and the firing order: units that didn't move fire first, with ¡Fusiles arriba!'s moved infantry (`unmovedGroup`), moved units wait)
  - `victory.ts`: winning a pass and play match (`Scenario.victory`: the medals to win and the objective hexes, each a side's, in the Allies' view): `heldObjectives`, `countMedals` (an enemy unit destroyed is a medal, from `GameSession.unitsLost`, the "remove" map edits of the turn log and this turn; an objective hex a medal while one of its side's units stands there) and `winnerOf` (at the end of a turn, the side that has reached the medals, the one with more if both have, none when level; a side with no units loses). `PassAndPlayMatch` scores at the end of each battle (`held`, `result`, saved), its snapshot's `victory` and `result`; `MatchView` gives every screen `VictoryContext`, read by `MedalTally` (the header, the reveal, the battle) and `Board` (the objective hexes' medals), and shows `MatchResultView` once the match is won: the final map of both armies with no fog (`MapOnly`), the medals and each side's stats (`matchStats.ts`' `sideStats`: dice rolled by face, shots, collisions, attack cards and cards played, from the turn log plus the turn still open, since the match ends before it's logged)
  - `turnLog.ts`: `recordTurn` turns a finished turn into a plain-JSON `TurnRecord` (card, orders, shots, map edits); `GameSession.endTurn` appends it to the log in the snapshot and save
  - `fireRules.ts`: engine for the "how many dice?" questionnaire (next question, adding up the dice)
  - `fireTargets.ts`: `hexDistance`, `hasLineOfSight` (blocked by terrain that `blocksSight` or this side's units in between; along an edge only if both hexes block) and `fireTargets`, the hexes in range with their dice (artillery fires without line of sight: `checksLineOfSight` in `unit.ts`); the map answers the distance and terrain questions (`mapAnswers`)
  - `rollResult.ts`: `readRoll` reads a roll against its target (hits, retreats, coins) with the rules in `data/hitRules.ts`
  - `coins.ts`: `turnCoins` works out the turn's coin (supplies) ledger from the card played, the shots (supply faces) and changes by hand, so undoing a shot gives its coins back; `GameSession` keeps the coins at the start of the turn plus `adjustCoins`/`undoCoinAdjustment`. A side starts with `STARTING_COINS` (`data/coinRules.ts`); the cards' costs are in `data/actionCards.ts`
  - `passAndPlay.ts`: `PassAndPlayMatch`, the pass and play mode (both sides on one device, the plan in [docs/oldPlans/PASS_AND_PLAY.md](docs/oldPlans/PASS_AND_PLAY.md), what's left in [docs/PASS_AND_PLAY_NEXT.md](docs/PASS_AND_PLAY_NEXT.md)): one `GameSession` per side, who holds the device and the cover screen; locking a side's orders hands the device over, and once both are locked the reveal (`RevealView`) shows both; `opponentOf` is the other army as a side may see it (orders hidden, units where they started, until revealed), drawn on every map through `OpponentContext`; after the reveal `moveUnits` carries out both sides' orders at once (`simultaneousMove.ts`: `moveSimultaneously`, one hex at a time in the Allies' view; units that meet an enemy stop short and collide; units of one side never hold each other up, only the hex each ends on counts, and when two end on one hex the later arrival goes back along its path, `settleSharedHexes`), cutting orders short with `GameSession.haltOrders`, and `rollCollision` rolls both units of a collision (`GameSession.rollCollision`: an ordered unit's shot, or a `standingCollisions` roll for a unit without an order; not for a unit whose order doesn't let it fire, nor one marked to retreat), then `applyCollision` deals both results at once to the units (with no flag on either side, a unit that moved goes one hex back along its path, `bounceBack`, through `haltOrders`; a unit that stood stays; a unit that rolled in a collision has no shots left in the battle, `summarizeOrders`) (`GameSession.sufferHits`: figures from `data/figures.ts`, kept between turns as the hits on each figure, front first and the squad leader last (`Figures`, `GameSession.figureHits`; `takeHits` hits the most damaged figure in front of the leader, the leader only once it's alone; `UnitComponent` marks the leader with a chevron) (armour: 3 figures of 2 hits; a figure hit but not lost is `UnitStrength.damaged`, its figures badge red; the hits each unit took this turn are `UnitStrength.hitThisTurn`, kept in `turnHits` and saved, cleared by `endTurn`, drawn as a red "−N" tag over the token, and a token hit since its map last drew it shakes once, `board__unit-hit`), pinned: a unit's first flag this turn pins it (`retreatFlags`, `isPinned`, `UnitStrength.pinned`; pinned infantry fires with a die less, `PINNED_FIRE_DICE`, `FireContext.pinned`, asked on own tablets with FireAim's switch, the `pinned` answer; a unit with an enemy next to it fires in close assault only, `FireContext.adjacentEnemy`, its targets cut to adjacent hexes, asked on own tablets with FireAim's switch, the `adjacentEnemy` answer), each flag after it is a hex to retreat at once (`retreatsDue`, `mustRetreat`) and takes it out of the battle (`hasRetreated`); limited damage: no more hits than figures, dropped automatically (`keptForHits`, `limitDamage`; on own tablets `ShotDice` asks how many figures fired, `keptForFigures`), flags and supplies always count (`keepResults` only drops hits));
