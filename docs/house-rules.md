# House rules (source notes)

These are the player's house rules for Memoir '44 with simultaneous turns, kept here as the source for the roadmap in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md). They were written a long time ago for pen and paper, not for an app, and may change. When a rule here is unclear or conflicts with another, **ask the player** before building it.

---

## General rules
- ~~When firing against artillery in close assault supplies count as a hit.~~ Changed (custom dice): the tank symbol hits armour and artillery alike, so a shot only asks whether the target is infantry, and a supply never hits.
- When using the card Dig-in 4 units can move 1 hex and place a sandbag.
- Gamble Command card: When picking up a card from the deck you can either keep it or decide to throw it away and pick a new card. This new card you must keep. If a probe card was used, you pick two cards up and choose between them instead (there are no recon cards in the smaller deck).
- When an infantry unit is left with one figure its fire power is max 2 dice after applying any dice modifications.
- Cards that have 'on the move' in standard map have 1 less on the move unit.
- *Experimental (a switch in the app's "Ajustes"):* when an artillery unit is destroyed, its crew stays on the hex as an infantry unit.

## Official rules the app doesn't apply
- **Not implemented:** armour that enters barbed wire removes it and can still fire (official). The app leaves the wire on the map: the tank fires as usual, and the wire is taken off the table and off the map with "Actualizar mapa". The rule book shows it flagged as not implemented.

## Changed units
Some units have new rules (some rules are still the same):
- Mobile artillery has 3 figures and when getting hit they are treated like tanks. Fire 3-3-2-2.
- Tigers can shoot 3-3-3-2 if they don't move. Re-rolls make a hit if a tank or a grenade appears. After the first hit they can only move one hex. Second hit dead.
- Jeeps have 2 figures, fire 3-2 and only grenades count as a hit.
- Half-Tracks fire 3-2 and can move 3 hexes.

## Simultaneous turns

### First turn in the game
The attacking side, being the side that would normally start, stated in the map overview, gets one extra turn at the start. This turn is played normally. The reasoning is that the starting side is the "attacking" side and their first move and attack is a surprise so the defenders should not have any chance to reply and certainly should not get the advantage of shooting first. In this extra turn the supplies rolled still earn coins, but nothing can be bought with them (no extra orders, no combat cards) until the next turn, and there is no end-of-turn reward.

He can't pick up combat cards or get coins though.

### Choosing cards
Each side at the same time chooses the card they want to play and the cards are not shown to the opponent.

If a player wants to use a combat card this turn he must place it face down on the table so that the opponent can see it. Remember only one combat card can be used per turn. You may still play reaction combat cards in the battle phase.

### Order phase
Record your wanted movements and orders on the small map. Movement indicated by arrows and firing orders with a F (or whatever). Once recorded and finished you can't change your mind.

If using a combat card record which unit or hex/s you are using it on, if necessary. Decisions can't be taken after seeing your opponent's map.

### Movement phase
Show your opponent your map and he shows his. Move all units marked with arrows to the corresponding hex and place a battle marker on units that have been ordered to battle. Pay the shown amount of coins if you want to use a combat card.

On the off chance that two units move through the same hex (even though they would end up on different hexes) or land on the same hex, they battle immediately. If the unit is capable of battling, roll your close assault dice minus 1 (normally 3-1 = 2) ignoring terrain battle restrictions and die reductions of the terrain. Retreats can't be ignored. A unit stays in the hex if the other is forced to retreat or gets eliminated and if he wants to keep moving up to the corresponding hex he may do so. If no retreats are rolled and no units retreat, then both units are forced to move one hex back following the path they just made. (The possibility of this hex being blocked does exist!)

### Battle phase
Each player will order a unit to fire after his opponent has fired, starting with the attacking player and following some specific rules.
- First all units that haven't moved fire.
- Then all of the units that have moved.

This means a player might fire twice with his units before the other player does. In other words, if there are units from both sides that will fire and haven't moved, the attacking player fires first and, by turns, both players finish firing all the units that haven't moved. Then do the same with the units that have moved.

Retreats are marked with a black marker and are taken into account at the end of the battle phase. If in close assault and a retreat is rolled the defender must ask the attacker if he wants to take ground (armor overrun) and only in this case is the retreat done immediately. The attacking unit must take ground. The unit forced to retreat may still battle from the new location. If a unit is marked for a retreat the unit can't take ground (armor overrun).

Any player can use a reaction combat card in the battling phase. Only one of these cards can be used by each player in the battle phase.

### Cards with changed rules
- **Barrage and airpower** (with no air rules): The player must mark the hex (barrage) or a line of adjacent hexes (airpower) that they plan to attack. Those hexes can have units on them, or can be empty. In the movement phase units may be ordered, by the other side, to enter or leave these hexes "marked for attack". If a unit, by chance, moves on to a hex they can be fired on. If a unit moves off such a hex, it receives no hits. With this new rule you can end up firing on not adjacent units.
- **Fire-fight:** Order up to 4 units to battle. If an enemy unit is adjacent to a unit firing, you don't roll your extra die and roll one less. Usually ends up being 2 dice.
- **Behind enemy lines:** The ordered unit fires and moves before any other attacks. Movement after the attack is done last, during the retreating phase.
- **Medics:** Spend 1 coin on every new infantry figure and 2 on any armor or artillery. Maximum figures you can get are 4.
- **Close assault:** You don't record anything on your map. Fire all units who are in close assault after all enemy units have moved.
- **Finest Hour:** The player may spend 1 coin to order an infantry unit and 2 coins to order armor or artillery. You are limited to 4 orders.

### Last phase
After all units have retreated, pick up a command card from the deck, attacking player first.

## New combat cards
Combat cards now have a new rule where you have to pay to use them. At the start you get 2 combat cards as usual.

Whenever you want to use a combat card in your hand you must pay the amount marked with a pen (powerful combat cards cost more). One extra unit can be ordered anywhere on the field costing 4 coins. Units ordered in this way do not benefit from anything that the main command card may have. This can be done in conjunction with a combat card and as many times as you want per turn. Remember to record the order on your map in the ordering phase.

If a new unit is to be placed on the field because of a combat card (Reinforcements) a cross must be drawn on the small map to indicate where it is going to appear.

Payment is done in the movement phase before any units are moved. When using a reaction to a battle combat card, payment is done immediately.

During battle, you get a coin for every supply you roll by dice used in combat, not any other rolls. If a supply counts as a hit because of any special rules, you do not get a coin.

At the end of each turn (in the last phase) when battling has finished and after picking up command cards, you can decide between picking a new combat card from the pile or picking up 2 coins. You can only have a maximum of 3 combat cards. If you already have 3 combat cards and still want to pick one up, you can substitute a combat card with one in your hand.

## Other house rules

### Limited damages
With a dice roll a unit can't keep more results than the number of figures in the unit. Example: an infantry unit of 2 figures attacks in close assault an infantry unit of 4 figures in a plain hex. It rolls 3 dice and gets 2 grenades and a flag. The active player must decide whether to keep one grenade and the flag or the two grenades.

Exception: cards that give bonus dice allow keeping one more die result. Example: an infantry unit of 2 figures attacks in close assault an enemy unit in a plain hex with the CLOSE ASSAULT card, so it rolls 4 dice and can keep 3 results.

### Grenades
~~A grenade is only a hit in close assault.~~ Changed (Step 20): a grenade is a hit at any range, as in official M44.

### Dice probability (design notes)
Close assault:
- Tank: 2/6
- Infantry: 3/6
- Artillery: 2/6

Firefight:
- Tank: 1/6 → one side (plus the other) → 1/4 tank
- Infantry: 2/6 → 3 sides → 3/8 2 infantry
- Artillery: 1/6 → 2 sides → 1/4 artillery
- Infantry on tank: 1/8 → one side → 1/8 grenade

An 8-sided die would look like: tank, grenade, inf, inf, inf, retreat, miss, miss. If infantry fires on a tank the grenade doesn't count. Used for long range. Maybe add an infantry figure on artillery if they seem weak.

**Built (custom dice):** the tank symbol hits any unit that isn't infantry (armour and artillery).
- Battle die (close assault, and at range without the long-range die): 2 infantry, tank, grenade, supply, flag. Infantry 3/6, anything else 2/6.
- Long-range die (8 sides, a per-game option, only at targets that aren't adjacent): 2 tanks, 3 infantry, flag, 2 supplies. Infantry 3/8, anything else 2/8 (also when infantry fires), retreat 1/8, supply 2/8.
- Attack cards' die (Barrage, Air Power, Air Bombardment): 2 infantry, tank, 2 grenades, flag (a grenade where the supply was, since supplies hit on these cards). Infantry 4/6, anything else 3/6.
- The app only asks "infantry or not" when the enemy starts the scenario with both (paratroopers count); it doesn't track the enemy's losses, and a reinforcement of the other kind is picked by hand ("Cambiar").

While programming make sure there is at least one side that is a coin and a retreat. Calculate for special units.
